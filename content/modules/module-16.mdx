## Medicare Parts A, B, C, and D

**Part A (Hospital):** Inpatient stays, limited skilled nursing (up to 100 days after 3-day hospital stay), hospice, some home health. Most people pay no premium for Part A.

**Part B (Medical):** Doctor visits, outpatient care, preventive services, medical supplies. Monthly premium $202.90 in 2026 (set by Medicare each year). Does NOT cover long-term care.

**Part C (Medicare Advantage):** Private plan replacing Parts A+B. Often includes dental, vision, hearing. Network restrictions (HMO/PPO). Lower premiums but potentially higher out-of-pocket when you need care.

**Part D (Prescriptions):** Covers prescription drugs. Standalone plan or included in Advantage. Coverage gap ("donut hole") used to apply. Part D now caps your yearly out-of-pocket drug costs, about $2,100 in 2026, and that cap adjusts each year.

Original Medicare (A+B) leaves you responsible for deductibles, copays, and coinsurance. That's where Medigap or Advantage fills in.

## Medigap vs. Medicare Advantage

**Medigap (Medicare Supplement):** Fills gaps in Original Medicare. See any Medicare-accepting doctor in the U.S. Higher premiums but predictable, minimal out-of-pocket costs. No referrals needed. Best for: chronic conditions, travel, desire for flexibility.

**Medicare Advantage (Part C):** Replaces Original Medicare with a private network plan. Lower premiums but network restrictions, potential referral requirements, and annual out-of-pocket maximums that can be $5,000-$8,000. Best for: healthy seniors on a tight budget who don't travel.

**RYAN'S REAL TALK:** Here's the decision simplified: if you have ongoing health issues and want to see any specialist without a referral, Medigap is worth the higher premium. If you're healthy and want the lowest monthly cost, Advantage works, but read the fine print on the network. The cheapest plan is only cheap until you need care outside the network.

## Medicaid: The Payer of Last Resort

Module 06 walks these rules in plain English with the full story behind them. This is the quick-reference version, with the numbers you will want to look up more than once.

### Eligibility

- Income limit: ~$2,982/month individual in 2026 (the 300%-of-SSI figure for nursing-home Medicaid; adjusts yearly, varies by state)
- Asset limit: ~$2,000 individual (excludes home, one vehicle, personal items)
- Must have medical need for care

### The 5-Year Lookback

Medicaid reviews all financial transactions for 5 years before application. Gifts, transfers below fair market value, and certain trust funding trigger penalty periods. This is why planning must start 5+ years before care is needed.

### Estate Recovery

After the Medicaid recipient dies, the state can seek reimbursement from the estate for care costs paid. This can include placing a lien on the home. An elder law attorney can help structure assets to minimize recovery.

### Spousal Protections

The community spouse (the healthy spouse) can keep the home, one vehicle, and a share of the couple's countable assets, in 2026 roughly $32,532 to $162,660 depending on the state, plus a monthly income floor between about $2,705 and $4,067. These figures adjust every year. Rules vary significantly by state, so always confirm current amounts with an elder law attorney.

## VA Aid & Attendance

Can provide roughly $1,558 a month for a surviving spouse up to about $2,424 a month for a veteran who needs daily help, and more for a veteran with a spouse. The amounts adjust every December 1, so verify the current figure.

### Who Qualifies

- Wartime veteran with 90+ days active duty (at least 1 day during wartime)
- Surviving spouse of qualifying veteran
- Must need help with activities of daily living
- Must meet income and asset limits (more generous than Medicaid)
- For late 2025 into 2026 the net-worth limit is about $163,699, and it adjusts yearly
- The VA has its own 3-year look-back on asset transfers, similar to Medicaid's 5-year rule, so do not start giving assets away to qualify without guidance

### Wartime Periods

- WWII: Dec 7, 1941 – Dec 31, 1946
- Korean War: June 27, 1950 – Jan 31, 1955
- Vietnam War: Nov 1, 1955 – May 7, 1975 for service in Vietnam; Aug 5, 1964 – May 7, 1975 for service elsewhere
- Gulf War: Aug 2, 1990 – present (open-ended until Congress sets an end date)

Use Tool 16B to check eligibility. If the senior is a veteran or surviving spouse, this benefit can fund significant portions of care. The application process is complex, so consider a VA-accredited attorney or claims agent. Help is also free through your county Veterans Service Officer, and you never pay a percentage of the benefit to apply, anyone who asks for that is a red flag.
