## 75% of Seniors Want to Stay Home. Here’s Why Most Can’t.

Over 75% of adults over 50 say they want to age in place — to stay in their own home for as long as possible. It’s the most common wish I hear from families. And it’s a beautiful goal when it’s realistic.

But here’s what nobody talks about: the home that was perfect at 55 can be dangerous at 75. Stairs become fall risks. Bathtubs become barriers. The nearest grocery store requires driving. And when the senior eventually needs daily help with bathing, dressing, or medications, the cost of in-home care can exceed $6,000-$20,000 per month — often more than assisted living.

__This module is not anti-aging-in-place. It’s pro-reality. If staying home is the right call, this module helps you do it safely. If it’s not the right call, this module helps you recognize that before a crisis forces the decision.__

__RYAN'S REAL TALK: __*As a house flipper, I’ve walked through hundreds of homes that were aging-in-place nightmares. Narrow hallways a wheelchair can’t navigate. Bathrooms with step-over tubs. Kitchens with overhead storage requiring step stools. The modifications needed to make these homes safe can cost $20,000-$100,000. Sometimes it makes more sense financially to sell the house and use that equity for senior living than to retrofit a home that was never designed for accessibility.*

## The Real Costs of Home Modifications

__Bathroom Safety ($5,000-$25,000\+): __Walk-in tub or curbless shower, grab bars (in studs), comfort-height toilet, non-slip flooring.

__Accessibility ($3,000-$50,000\+): __Stairlift ($3,000-$8,000), wheelchair ramp ($1,000-$8,000), widening doorways ($1,000-$5,000 per door), home elevator ($15,000-$50,000).

__Kitchen & Laundry ($2,500-$15,000): __Lowering countertops, pull-down shelving, front-load washer/dryer, lever faucet handles.

__General Safety ($1,000-$10,000): __Improved lighting, non-slip flooring throughout, smart home technology (medical alerts, video doorbells, automated lighting).

__These are not one-time costs. As needs change, further modifications are required. A home safe for a walker may not be safe for a wheelchair. Use Tool 14B to assess your specific home.__

## The Hidden Cost: In-Home Care

The biggest hidden cost of aging in place is the eventual need for professional care.

__Companion Care ($28-$35/hour): __Socialization, errands, light housekeeping. Not medical.

__Personal Care ($30-$40/hour): __Help with bathing, dressing, eating, toileting. The care most seniors eventually need.

__Skilled Nursing ($50-$100\+/hour): __Licensed nurse for medical needs: wound care, injections, complex medications.

__At 44 hours per week of personal care (a common amount), the national median cost is over $6,000 per month. For round-the-clock care, costs can exceed $20,000 per month — far more than most assisted living communities.__

Use Tool 14A (Cost Calculator) to compare the 5-year total cost of aging in place vs. moving to senior living. The numbers often surprise families.

__RYAN'S REAL TALK: __*Here’s the math most families don’t do: if Mom needs 6 hours of personal care per day at $35/hour, that’s $6,300 per month. Add mortgage, utilities, home maintenance, and modifications, and aging in place costs $8,000-$10,000 per month. Assisted living with personal care included might be $5,000-$7,000 per month. Aging in place can actually be more expensive, not less.*

## When Aging in Place Is NOT the Right Choice

•  Progressive disease (Alzheimer’s, Parkinson’s, ALS) requiring escalating care levels

•  Home is structurally unsuitable for modification (multi-story, narrow hallways)

•  Senior is isolated — no nearby friends, family, or community activities

•  Financial resources can’t support both modifications and ongoing in-home care

•  Family caregivers are burned out or unavailable

•  Safety incidents are increasing (falls, forgotten medications, wandering)

__If 3\+ of these apply, aging in place may not be viable long-term. Having a Plan B ready prevents crisis-mode decisions.__

## Creating Your Plan B

Even if aging in place is your preferred strategy, you need a backup plan.

__Reassess every 6-12 months: __Use the Stage Assessment from Module 1 to track changes in health, safety, and independence.

__Research alternatives in advance: __Tour communities, understand costs, get on waiting lists. Don’t start from scratch during a crisis.

__Define trigger points: __What specific events would signal it’s time to move? A fall with injury? Hospitalization 3\+ times in 6 months? Getting lost? Write these down and agree as a family.

__Financial plan: __How will you pay for alternative care? Home sale equity? LTC insurance? Medicaid?

__Family agreement: __Everyone agrees on the criteria for when a transition will happen. This prevents arguments driven by guilt or denial.

Use Tool 14C (Plan B Timeline) to document all of this.
