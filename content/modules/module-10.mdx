## Moving Day Doesn't Have to Be Chaos

Moving day is where all your planning either pays off or falls apart. I've seen both. The families who have a plan execute a calm, organized move in 6-8 hours. The families who wing it spend 12 hours in chaos, forget essential items, and end the day in tears.

This module turns you into a project manager. The same discipline I used to coordinate construction crews, deliveries, and inspections on house flips applies directly to move management. Different tools, same principles: timeline, checklist, delegation, and a contingency plan.

**RYAN'S REAL TALK:** The single most important piece of advice I give every family: pack the Move Day Essentials Box first, load it in your car last, and do not let it go on the moving truck. This box contains medications, documents, glasses, phone chargers, and comfort items. It's your lifeline for the first 24 hours. If the moving truck gets delayed, lost, or the new home isn't ready, this box keeps everyone safe and functional.

## The 4-Week Move Timeline

### 4 Weeks Before

- Book movers or senior move manager
- Confirm move-in date with new community
- Schedule utility disconnections and connections
- Order packing supplies
- Start packing non-essential items

### 3 Weeks Before

- Continue packing non-essentials (seasonal items, books, extra linens)
- Label ALL boxes: room + contents
- Submit change of address with USPS (do this online at usps.com)
- Schedule post-move cleaning for old home

### 2 Weeks Before

- Pack majority of belongings, leave only daily essentials out
- Confirm move date and arrival time with movers
- Transfer prescriptions to pharmacy near new home
- Arrange pet care for move day

### 1 Week Before

- Pack everything except Move Day Essentials Box and First Night Box
- Defrost and clean refrigerator
- Final walkthrough of new home to verify readiness
- Label rooms in new home with signs so movers know where boxes go
- Confirm movers one final time

## Professional Movers vs. Senior Move Manager

**Professional Movers ($1,500-$5,000):** Load, transport, and unload. You handle packing, unpacking, and coordination. Best when you have local family to manage the process.

**Senior Move Manager ($3,000-$10,000+):** Full-service: packing, hiring movers, unpacking, setting up the new home, disposing of unwanted items. Best for out-of-state families, complex moves, or when you want completely hands-off.

A senior move manager is the most underrated investment in the transition. They're project managers who specialize in reducing stress. The new home is set up, bed made, pictures hung, coffee maker ready, before the senior walks in.

**RYAN'S REAL TALK:** If your family lives out of state, hire a senior move manager. It's not optional. Trying to coordinate a move remotely with a moving company, utility transfers, cleaners, and a stressed parent on the other end of the phone is a recipe for disaster. The $5,000-$8,000 for a move manager saves you $2,000 in plane tickets, a week of vacation time, and your sanity.

## How to Vet a Senior Move Manager

A senior move manager can be worth every dollar, but the title is not regulated, so anyone can hang out a shingle. Before you hand someone the keys and a lifetime of belongings, check them out like you would any contractor coming into the home.

- Look for membership in the National Association of Senior Move Managers (NASMM). Members sign on to a code of ethics and carry the training that separates a pro from a helper with a truck.
- Confirm they are insured and bonded, and that their workers are background-checked. This person and their crew will be alone with your parent's valuables.
- Get a written estimate that spells out exactly what is included: packing, moving, unpacking, setting up the new place, and clearing out what stays behind. Vague hourly promises are where bills balloon.
- Ask for references from other families they have moved, and actually call them.

Trust, but verify. The good ones are organized, insured, and happy to hand you references. Anyone who dodges those questions, or wants cash up front with no written scope, is telling you what you need to know.

## The Move Day Essentials Box + First Night Box

### Move Day Essentials Box (Goes in YOUR Car)

- **Medications:** Full week's supply, prescription list, pharmacy contact info
- **Documents:** IDs, insurance cards, POAs, contact lists, new home paperwork
- **Personal:** Eyeglasses, hearing aids, phone + charger, toiletries, change of clothes
- **Comfort:** Favorite snacks, bottled water, familiar blanket
- **Logistics:** Keys to both homes, cash, credit cards

### First Night Box (Goes in Your Car Too)

- **Bedding:** Sheets, pillows, blankets for the first night
- **Bathroom:** Towels, toilet paper, soap, toothbrush/toothpaste
- **Kitchen:** Coffee maker, mugs, snacks, paper plates, basic utensils
- **Comfort:** 2-3 familiar photos or a favorite throw to make the space feel like home immediately

## Move Day: A Project Manager's Approach

### The Day Before

- Label each room in new home with signs
- Protect floors with cardboard or plastic
- Confirm movers' arrival time
- Charge all phones and devices

### Move Day

- Designate ONE person to direct movers at each location
- Final walkthrough of old home, check every closet, cabinet, and storage area
- Take photos of any pre-existing damage to furniture
- Move Day Essentials Box and First Night Box go in YOUR car, not the truck

### First 24 Hours at New Home

**Priority 1, Bedroom:** Make the bed with fresh linens. A place to rest.

**Priority 2, Bathroom:** Unpack toiletries, towels, medications. Safety first.

**Priority 3, Kitchen:** Coffee maker, mugs, snacks, basic utensils.

**Priority 4, Comfort:** Hang a few photos, place the familiar blanket. Home.

Do NOT try to unpack everything on day one. The goal is safe, comfortable, and familiar. Everything else can wait.

## What to Do With Everything That Is Not Going

Here is the part families underestimate every time: a smaller place cannot hold a lifetime of belongings, and deciding what happens to the rest is often the hardest, most emotional job in the whole move. Give it time, and give the senior a say. Rushing this is how feelings get hurt and things that mattered get lost.

### Sort Into Five Piles

Everything in the house falls into one of five buckets: goes with them to the new place, goes to family, gets sold, gets donated, or gets tossed. Start with the keepsakes. The photos, the letters, the few pieces that carry the story of a life get protected and delivered to the people who should have them, not swept out with the rest. That respect matters more than any dollar figure.

### Selling the Rest

For a full house, an estate sale company will price and sell the contents over a weekend, usually for a cut of the take, often somewhere from a third to nearly half. That can be worth it to clear a house fast, but vet them the same as a move manager: get the terms in writing, agree on how things get priced, and settle up front what happens to whatever does not sell. For a few higher-value pieces, consignment or an online marketplace may net more if someone has the time and patience to manage it.

### Donating and Clearing Out

Reputable charities will take furniture and household goods, and many will pick up. Get a written receipt for anything donated, it may help at tax time. Whatever is truly junk, a haul-away service clears in an afternoon. One caution: be wary of anyone offering to clear the whole house for free. Free often means they keep everything of value and the family never sees a dime of what it was worth.

**RYAN'S REAL TALK:** On my flips I learned this the hard way: the stuff is not just stuff to your parent. When the keepsakes matter, you protect them and get them to the family they belong to, even if that means driving loads of them across the state so nothing that matters gets lost. That is the standard. Handle the sentimental things with care and take your time on them. The furniture and the housewares, move those quicker, so the emotional energy goes where it counts.
