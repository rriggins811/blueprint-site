## The Finish Line Is Here

If you're reading this module, you've done something remarkable. You've decluttered a lifetime of possessions. Sorted and rightsized with care. Prepared the home. Secured the legal and financial foundation. Chosen a community. Planned and executed a move. That is months of work, emotional labor, and family coordination.

This module is the final stretch. The walkthrough, the closing, and the handoff. After this, the house chapter is closed and the next chapter begins.

## The Final Walkthrough

Complete this 24-48 hours before closing. Use Tool 11B to check every item.

### Every Room

- All personal belongings removed
- Walls free of nails, holes patched
- Light fixtures and ceiling fans working
- Windows clean and operational
- Floors swept and mopped

### Kitchen

- All appliances clean and working
- Cabinets and drawers empty
- Refrigerator defrosted and cleaned
- Sink and counters clean

### Bathrooms

- All personal items removed
- Toilets, sinks, tubs clean
- Medicine cabinets empty

### Garage / Basement / Attic

- Completely empty
- Floors swept
- No items left behind

### Exterior

- Yard mowed and edged
- Walkways clear
- Trash cans removed
- Mailbox empty

Take photos of every room after the walkthrough. This protects you if the buyer claims something was left behind or damaged.

## Handling Last-Minute Issues

### Buyer Requests Additional Repairs

- Review the purchase agreement, what are you obligated to fix?
- Get your own contractor's quote, don't accept the buyer's number at face value
- Negotiate: offer a closing credit instead of making repairs
- Consult your agent before agreeing to anything

### Inspection Reveals Unexpected Issues

- Don't panic, most issues are negotiable
- Get your own assessment of severity and cost
- Consider a price reduction instead of repairs
- Remember: the buyer wants to close too

### Closing Date Needs to Change

- Communicate immediately with all parties
- Understand the financial impact (carrying costs, storage)
- Get the change in writing
- Adjust your move timeline accordingly

## Closing Day

### What to Bring

- Photo ID (driver's license or passport)
- Proof of homeowners insurance cancellation
- Final utility bills showing accounts closed
- All house keys, garage openers, access codes
- Appliance manuals and warranties
- Checkbook for last-minute adjustments

### What to Expect

Closing typically takes 1-2 hours. You'll sign numerous documents. Bring reading glasses. Funds are usually wired to your account same day or next business day. Ask the closing attorney when exactly to expect funds.

## Protecting Your Money at the Closing Table

You are about to move a large sum of money, often the biggest check of your parent's life, and the scammers know it. Real estate closings are a favorite target for wire fraud, and the seller's proceeds are exactly what they are after. A few minutes of caution here protects everything you have worked for through this whole process.

### The Wire-Fraud Scam, and How to Beat It

Here is how it works. Somewhere in the final days before closing, an email arrives that looks like it is from the closing attorney, the title company, or the agent, with updated wire instructions for where your proceeds should go. The account belongs to the scammer. By the time anyone notices, the money is gone and it is very hard to get back. The rule that stops this cold: never trust wiring instructions, or any change to them, that arrive by email or text. Before a dime moves, call the closing attorney or title company at a phone number you looked up yourself, not one from the email, and confirm out loud exactly where your money is going and how you will receive it. If instructions change at the last minute, treat it as a red flag until you have verified it by phone with a person you know.

### Know How the Money Actually Comes

At closing you will get a settlement statement, sometimes called the closing disclosure, that lays out the sale price, the payoff of any mortgage or lien, the closing costs, and the net amount you walk away with. Read it before you sign, and make sure the mortgage payoff and any liens match what you expected. Proceeds usually come by wire the same day or the next business day, or sometimes by check. Ask the closing attorney to tell you, in person, the exact amount and the exact date to expect it, then confirm the money actually lands in the account you verified.

**RYAN'S REAL TALK:** I tell every family the same thing before closing: if anyone emails you new wire instructions, assume it is a scam until you have called and confirmed with a real person at the closing office. I have seen sellers lose their entire proceeds to a fake email that looked perfect. Two minutes on the phone protects the whole payday. Make the call.

## The Emotional Side

Selling the family home is a milestone. It's normal to feel relief, sadness, anxiety, and gratitude all at once. Ways to honor the transition:

- Take final photos of each room before the walkthrough
- Write down favorite memories
- Share stories with family
- Focus on what's ahead: the new chapter, the new community, the new beginning

## Post-Closing Tasks

### Immediately After Closing

- ☐ Confirm funds received in bank account
- ☐ Cancel homeowners insurance
- ☐ Confirm all utilities transferred/closed
- ☐ File closing documents in safe place

### Within 7 Days

- ☐ Update address with any remaining accounts
- ☐ Forward any stray mail from old address
- ☐ Notify remaining contacts of new address

### Within 30 Days

- ☐ Report sale to tax preparer/CPA
- ☐ Calculate capital gains (if applicable)
- ☐ Update estate planning documents if needed
- ☐ Review financial plan with sale proceeds

### Within 90 Days

- ☐ Complete Loops 30-day check-in (Module 12)
- ☐ Verify all subscriptions and accounts updated
- ☐ Confirm no outstanding bills from old address
