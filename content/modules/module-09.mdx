## The 6 Exit Strategies

Most agents know one way to sell: list on the MLS and hope. You have six options.

**1. Traditional MLS Listing:**Highest potential price. Requires prep, showings, 60-90 days. Best for homes in decent condition with flexible timelines.

**2. As-Is Cash Offer:**Speed and simplicity. No repairs, no showings, close in 7-21 days. Typically 70-85% of market value. Best for urgent timelines or homes needing major work.

**3. Owner Financing:**You become the bank. Buyer makes monthly payments to you instead of a mortgage company. Creates ongoing income stream. Risk: buyer defaults.

**4. Lease-Option (Rent-to-Own):**Generate rental income while waiting for market improvement. Tenant has option to buy later. Risk: you remain a landlord.

**5. 1031 Exchange:**Defer all capital gains taxes by reinvesting proceeds into another property within 180 days. Complex rules. Must identify replacement property within 45 days.

**6. Keep as Rental:**Generate passive income and build long-term wealth. You remain a landlord with all associated responsibilities.

**95% of senior transition families will use Strategy 1 or 2. But knowing all six ensures you’re never forced into a box that doesn’t fit.**

## Traditional vs. As-Is: The Real Comparison

## Traditional MLS Listing

•  Highest sale price potential

•  Full market exposure, multiple offers possible

•  Requires prep work and the $5,000 Smart Prep Package

•  Showings can be disruptive to the senior

•  60-90 day process (prep + listing + closing)

•  Subject to inspection, appraisal, and buyer financing contingencies

## As-Is Cash Offer

•  No repairs, no cleaning, no showings

•  Leave unwanted items behind

•  Close in 7-21 days

•  Guaranteed close — no financing contingency

•  Lower price (typically 70-85% of market value)

•  Minimal closing costs ($500-$1,500)

## The Hybrid Approach

Do light preparation and list traditionally while simultaneously collecting cash backup offers. If the market listing doesn’t produce results in 30-45 days, you have a guaranteed cash exit ready.

## The Decision Pyramid

Work through these five questions in order. Each answer narrows your path.

**Level 1 — Timeline:**How quickly do you need funds? Under 45 days = as-is likely best. 6+ months = traditional has time to work.

**Level 2 — Home Condition:**Light cosmetic work needed? Traditional. $15K+ in repairs? As-is or hybrid.

**Level 3 — Stress Tolerance:**Can the senior handle showings and strangers in the home? If not, as-is removes that entirely.

**Level 4 — Financial Priority:**Is maximum price critical for funding care, or is certainty and speed more valuable?

**Level 5 — Available Support:**Do you have local family to manage contractors and showings? If not, as-is or hire a move manager.

Use Tool 9D (Decision Pyramid Assessment) to walk through this systematically.

## Calculating Net Proceeds

**Never compare sale prices. Always compare net proceeds — what you actually walk away with after all costs.**

## Traditional Sale Costs

•  Real estate commission: 5-6% of sale price

•  Repairs and updates: $5,000-$15,000

•  Staging: $1,000-$3,000

•  Closing costs: $2,000-$5,000

•  Carrying costs while on market: mortgage + utilities + insurance per month

## Cash Sale Costs

•  Closing costs: $500-$1,500

•  No repairs, no staging, no carrying costs

Example: $300,000 market value home. Traditional net after all costs: ~$270,000. Cash offer at 80%: $240,000 net. Difference: $30,000. The question: is $30,000 worth 60-90 days of showings, repairs, and stress? For some families yes. For others, absolutely not.

**WHOLESALER WARNING:**Be extremely careful with “we buy houses” companies. Many are wholesalers who put your home under contract at 50-60% of value, then flip the contract to an investor for profit. You net far less than a legitimate cash buyer. Never sign anything without reading it. Module content in The Other Side of the Conversation covers this in depth.

## Working with Agents and Evaluating Cash Offers

## Choosing an Agent (Traditional Path)

•  How many senior transition sales have you handled?

•  What’s your average days on market for this price range?

•  What repairs do you recommend and why?

•  How will you minimize disruption to the senior?

•  What’s your commission and is it negotiable?

•  Can you provide references from similar situations?

## Evaluating Cash Offers

•  Get offers from at least 2-3 cash buyers to compare

•  Ask for proof of funds before signing anything

•  Check if the buyer is an end investor or a wholesaler — ask directly

•  Compare net proceeds, not just the offer price

•  Have your attorney review any contract before signing

•  Never accept an “assignment” clause without understanding it
