## The 5 Essential Documents

These five documents form the foundation of every estate plan. Without them, your family is legally powerless to help.

## 1. Will or Living Trust

A will directs asset distribution after death through probate (court-supervised, public, 6-18 months). A revocable living trust does the same but avoids probate entirely — private, faster, and less expensive in the long run. For most homeowners, a trust is the better tool.

**Will:**$500-$1,500 to create. Goes through probate. Public record. Does nothing during incapacity.

**Trust:**$2,500-$5,000+ to create. Avoids probate. Private. Your successor trustee can manage assets if you become incapacitated. Must be “funded” — assets must be retitled into the trust for it to work.

## 2. Durable Power of Attorney (Financial)

Allows your chosen agent to manage finances if you become incapacitated. Pay bills, manage investments, handle real estate, file taxes. Without it: court-supervised guardianship ($5,000-$15,000). Expires at death.

## 3. Healthcare Power of Attorney & Living Will

Allows your agent to make medical decisions. The living will portion states your end-of-life wishes (life support, resuscitation, feeding tubes). Without it: doctors may not be able to discuss treatment with family during emergencies. Expires at death.

## 4. HIPAA Authorization

Specifically authorizes healthcare providers to share medical information with designated family members. Even with a Healthcare POA, some providers require a separate HIPAA release. Having both prevents delays.

## 5. Beneficiary Designations

**Not a document you create with an attorney — these are on file at your bank, investment company, and insurance provider. Beneficiary designations on IRAs, 401(k)s, and life insurance override your will and trust. An ex-spouse can inherit everything if you forget to update these. Review annually.**

**RYAN'S REAL TALK:** *The #1 estate planning mistake I see: a family creates a beautiful trust, pays $4,000 for it, and then never retitles their assets into it. A trust that doesn’t own anything is an empty bucket. Make sure your attorney walks you through the funding process and verifies that the house, bank accounts, and investment accounts are titled in the trust’s name.*

## The 5 Most Costly Mistakes

**Mistake 1 — Procrastinating:**Once capacity is lost, it’s too late to create these documents. A stroke, fall, or sudden illness can happen without warning. Schedule the attorney appointment this week.

**Mistake 2 — Using DIY Online Documents:**Online forms are generic, often not state-specific, and frequently executed improperly. An invalid document is worse than no document — it creates a false sense of security.

**Mistake 3 — Not Funding the Trust:**A trust that doesn’t own assets doesn’t protect anything. The house deed, bank accounts, and investments must be retitled into the trust.

**Mistake 4 — Outdated Beneficiary Designations:**These override your will and trust. Ex-spouses, deceased individuals, or minor children as beneficiaries cause expensive legal problems.

**Mistake 5 — Choosing the Wrong Decision-Makers:**Your executor and POA agents need to be trustworthy, organized, financially responsible, and available. It’s not an honorary title — it’s a job. Use Tool 8D to evaluate candidates objectively.

## Digital Assets and Your Estate

Your digital life is part of your estate. Email accounts, social media profiles, online banking, cloud-stored photos, subscriptions, and cryptocurrency. Without a plan, your family may be locked out of accounts containing irreplaceable photos, financial records, or ongoing subscriptions that keep charging.

## What to Include in Your Digital Asset Inventory

•  Email accounts (Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook)

•  Social media (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn)

•  Online banking and financial accounts

•  Cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox, iCloud)

•  Subscription services (Netflix, Amazon, streaming)

•  Digital photo libraries

•  Password manager credentials (if you use one)

•  Cryptocurrency wallets and keys

**Store this inventory securely. Tell your executor exactly where to find it. Update it annually. Use Tool 8B for the complete template.**

## Choosing the Right Decision-Makers

The people you choose for these roles will have enormous power over your finances, healthcare, and legacy. Choose carefully.

## Executor / Successor Trustee

Manages the estate after death. Pays debts, distributes assets, files final tax returns. Must be organized, detail-oriented, and able to handle pressure from other family members. Choosing the oldest child by default is a mistake if they’re not the most capable.

## Financial Power of Attorney Agent

Manages finances during incapacity. Access to bank accounts, authority to pay bills and manage investments. Must be trustworthy with money and available to act quickly when needed. Consider naming a professional fiduciary if no family member is suitable.

## Healthcare Power of Attorney Agent

Makes medical decisions during incapacity. Must understand the senior’s wishes, be willing to advocate with doctors, and be emotionally capable of making difficult end-of-life decisions.
